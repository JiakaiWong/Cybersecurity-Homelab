## What is a " Kill Chain 

#### Question: Where does the term "Kill Chain" originate from?
**Answer:**  `Military`

**Explanation:**
The concept of a Kill Chain originates from the military, where it describes the structured sequence of actions used to identify, target, and neutralize threats. 
In cybersecurity, the same concept is applied to describe the attacker’s steps from reconnaissance to the achievement of their objectives. 
Understanding these steps allows defenders to identify weak points and disrupt the attacker before they can complete their mission.

## Threat Modelling


Threat modelling, in a cybersecurity context, is a series of steps to ultimately improve the security of a system. Threat modelling is about identifying risk and essentially boils down to:

1. Identifying what systems and applications need to be secured and what function they serve in the environment. For example, is the system critical to normal operations, and is a system holding sensitive information like payment info or addresses?
2. Assessing what vulnerabilities and weaknesses these systems and applications may have and how they could be potentially exploited
3. Creating a plan of action to secure these systems and applications from the vulnerabilities highlighted
4. Putting in policies to prevent these vulnerabilities from occurring again where possible (for example, implementing a software development life cycle (SDLC) for an application or training employees on phishing awareness).

#### Question: What is the technical term for a piece of software or hardware in IT (Information Technology?)
**Answer:**  `Asset`

**Explanation:**
Threat modelling is an important procedure in reducing the risk within a system or application, as it creates a high-level overview of an organisation's IT assets (an asset in IT is a piece of software or hardware) and the procedures to resolve vulnerabilities.

#### Question: In what year was the Unified Kill Chain framework released?
**Answer:**  `2017`

**Explanation:**
The Unified Kill Chain was introduced in 2017 by Paul Pols. It was created to integrate and enhance previous models like Lockheed Martin’s Cyber Kill Chain and the MITRE ATT&CK framework.

#### Question: According to the Unified Kill Chain, how many phases are there to an attack?
**Answer:**  `18`

**Explanation:**
The Unified Kill Chain defines 18 distinct attack phases. These phases cover every part of an adversary’s lifecycle, providing more detailed visibility than earlier frameworks.

#### Question: What is the name of the attack phase where an attacker employs techniques to evade detection?
**Answer:**  `Defense Evasion`

**Explanation:**
Defense Evasion includes the attacker’s attempts to hide their activities from security tools, logs, or analysts. This can involve disabling antivirus programs, clearing logs, or using obfuscation techniques to remain undetected.

#### Question: What is the name of the attack phase where an attacker employs techniques to remove data from a network?
**Answer:**  `Exfiltration`

**Explanation:**
Exfiltration is the phase in which attackers transfer stolen data from the compromised environment to a system they control. It represents the culmination of many attacks where sensitive data, such as credentials or intellectual property, is extracted.


#### Question: What is the name of the attack phase where an attacker achieves their objectives?
**Answer:**  `Objectives`

**Explanation:**
The Objectives phase is the attacker’s end goal, which can vary from stealing data, causing disruption, encrypting systems for ransom, or maintaining long-term espionage.

## Goal: In (Initial Foothold)
<img width="1201" height="844" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b2936dd3-26a2-4e41-a5c5-9931adf9a43b" />

#### Question: What is an example of a tactic to gain a foothold using emails?
**Answer:**  `Phishing`

**Explanation:**
Phishing is one of the most common initial access methods. Attackers send fraudulent emails designed to trick users into revealing credentials or downloading malicious attachments.

#### Question: Impersonating an employee to request a password reset is a form of what?
**Answer:**  `Social Engineering`

**Explanation:**
Social Engineering involves manipulating human behavior to bypass security. By pretending to be an authorized person, attackers can deceive others into revealing confidential information or performing risky actions.

#### Question: An adversary setting up the Command & Control server is what phase of the Unified Kill Chain?
**Answer:**  `Weaponization`

**Explanation:**
During Weaponization, attackers prepare their infrastructure and tools before launching the attack. This includes setting up command-and-control servers, creating malicious payloads, and testing exploits.

#### Question: Exploiting a vulnerability present on a system is what phase of the Unified Kill Chain?
**Answer:**  `Exploitation`

**Explanation:**
Exploitation refers to the moment an attacker takes advantage of a weakness in software or configuration to execute unauthorized code or gain access.

#### Question: Moving from one system to another is an example of?
**Answer:**  `Pivoting`

**Explanation:**
Pivoting occurs when attackers move laterally through the network to expand their access, typically after compromising an initial system.


#### Question: Leaving behind a malicious service that allows the adversary to log back into the target is what?
**Answer:**  `Persistence`

**Explanation:**
Persistence techniques ensure the attacker retains access to a system even after reboots or partial remediation. This may involve creating scheduled tasks, installing backdoors, or modifying registry entries.

## Goal: Through (Network Propagtion)

#### Question: As a SOC analyst, you pick up an alert pointing to failed logins from an administrator account.
What phase of the Unified Kill Chain would an attacker be seeking to achieve?
**Answer:**  `Privilege Escalation`


**Explanation:**
Privilege Escalation allows attackers to gain higher-level permissions on a system, enabling deeper access and control. Repeated failed logins suggest brute-force or credential abuse attempts to obtain administrator privileges.

#### Question: Mimikatz, a known post-exploitation tool, was detected on the IT Manager's workstation.
The Security logs show that the tool was attempting to dump OS and user secrets.
Which Unified Kill Chain phase does this activity correspond to?
**Answer:**  `Credential Access`

**Explanation:**
Credential Dumping refers to extracting passwords or hashes from memory or databases. Tools like Mimikatz target Windows authentication stores to gather credentials that can be reused elsewhere in the network.

## Goal: Out (Action on Objective)

<img width="1197" height="771" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1d1939fe-129a-424f-a5a9-4d56e4e21afe" />

#### Question: While monitoring the network as a SOC analyst, you observe a big traffic spike.
Most of the network traffic is sent to an unknown, suspicious IP address.
What Unified Kill Chain phase could describe this activity?
**Answer:**  `Exfiltration`

Explanation:
Outbound data flow to unknown destinations is a classic symptom of Exfiltration, where attackers move stolen data from internal systems to their external command centers or servers.

#### Question: Personally identifiable information (PII) has been released to the public by an adversary.
Your organisation is facing reputational losses and scrutiny for the breach.
What part of the CIA triad would be affected by this action?
**Answer:**  `Confidentiality`

In the CIA Triad (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability), this breach impacts Confidentiality, meaning unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information.

Conclusion: 
The Unified Kill Chain offers a comprehensive framework for understanding and mitigating cyber attacks. Unlike earlier models, it covers all 18 stages of an attack lifecycle, connecting early reconnaissance to final objectives.

Key Lessons Learned:
- Identifying which phase an attacker is in allows defenders to implement phase-specific countermeasures.
- Phishing and social engineering remain dominant initial access vectors; continuous user education and email filtering are essential.
- Detection of persistence mechanisms and privilege escalation attempts is critical for early containment.
- Monitoring outbound traffic helps detect exfiltration and data breaches.
- Combining frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK with the Unified Kill Chain provides full-spectrum visibility into attack behaviors.
